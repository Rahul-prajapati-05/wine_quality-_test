Exploratory Data Analysis

Statistical Visualizations & Oenological Insights

Dive into the empirical patterns of the Red Wine Quality dataset. Generated using Seaborn & Matplotlib at 300 DPI.

Core Finding

Severe Class Imbalance

Quality ratings 5 and 6 account for 82.49% of the 1,599 samples. Extreme ratings (3 and 8) constitute only 0.63% and 1.13% respectively.

Key Takeaways:

  • Sensory panelists naturally gravitate toward median scores during blind tastings.
  • Standard accuracy is deceptive: a dummy classifier predicting Class 5/6 achieves 82.5% accuracy but zero utility.
  • This justified engineering the 3 commercial tiers: Low (3–4), Medium (5–6), and High (7–8).
Feature Interaction

Correlation Matrix Heatmap

Visualizes Pearson correlation coefficients across all 11 physicochemical attributes and wine quality ratings.

Key Takeaways:

  • Alcohol (+0.48): Most prominent positive correlation with quality.
  • Volatile Acidity (-0.39): Most prominent negative correlation. Acetic acid directly impairs flavor.
  • Sulphates (+0.25): Positive correlation due to antioxidant stability.
  • Fixed Acidity vs Density (+0.67): Collinear relationship handled cleanly by tree ensembles.
Biochemical Driver

Alcohol vs. Wine Quality

Boxplot analysis with mean markers across every individual rating score from 3 to 8.

Key Takeaways:

  • Quality 3 and 4 wines average ~9.9% alcohol by volume.
  • Quality 7 and 8 wines average ~11.5% to 12.1% alcohol.
  • Higher alcohol signals ripe, physiologically mature grapes with concentrated sugars, yielding fuller mouthfeel and balanced tannins.
Data Distributions

Physicochemical Distributions

Histograms with Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) overlays for all 11 chemical features.

Key Takeaways:

  • Gaussian Distributions: pH (mean 3.31) and density (mean 0.9967) display clean normal curves.
  • Right-Skewed Attributes: Residual sugar, chlorides, and total sulfur dioxide exhibit heavy right tails.
  • Random Forest is unaffected by skewness due to monotonic decision splitting.
Data Quality

Interquartile Range Outliers

Boxplots isolating statistical outliers beyond 1.5 × IQR for all chemical variables.

Key Takeaways:

  • Extreme outliers detected in residual sugar (up to 15.5 g/dm³) and total SO₂ (up to 289 mg/dm³).
  • These represent genuine high-sweetness or heavily stabilized production batches rather than measurement errors.
  • Retaining them allows models to learn boundary behaviors without data loss.

Chemical Drivers of Wine Excellence

Gini impurity feature importances extracted directly from the Random Forest model.

01

Alcohol Content 15.20% Share

Primary positive contributor. Higher ethanol enhances body, viscous tears on the glass, perceived sweetness, and masks astringent tannins.

02

Volatile Acidity 14.81% Share

Primary negative contributor. High acetic acid imparts pungent vinegar notes, indicating spoilage or stuck fermentation.

03

Sulphates 13.88% Share

Essential antimicrobial additive. Acts as a preservative protecting fresh fruit aromas against oxidative browning and degradation.

04

Total Sulfur Dioxide 8.27% Share

Balances active free SO₂ to maintain bacterial defense while avoiding sulfurous pungency in bottle.
